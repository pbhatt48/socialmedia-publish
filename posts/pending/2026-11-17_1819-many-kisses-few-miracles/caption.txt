"We’ve observed many kisses but very few miracles." — Warren Buffett, 1981 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett’s fairy tale: acquirers pay double for a mediocre business, confident their managerial “kiss” will transform it.
Shareholders could buy the same toad themselves at the going price for toads. The premium only pays off if the kiss works.
It rarely does. Most of the premium is paid up front, and the transformation is assumed rather than earned.
When a company announces a large deal, ask what it is paying for: the business as it is, or the business management hopes to make it.

Which acquisition do you think destroyed the most shareholder value, and what did management promise at the time?
