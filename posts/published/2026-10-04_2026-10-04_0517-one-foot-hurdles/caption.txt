"Charlie and I have not learned how to solve difficult business problems. What we have learned is to avoid them." — Warren Buffett, 1989 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this after 25 years of buying and supervising businesses, in a section on his own mistakes.
In the same passage he credits Berkshire's success to finding one-foot hurdles it could step over, not to any ability to clear seven-footers.
The skill being described is selection, not heroics. Most of the edge comes before the purchase, in deciding what not to attempt.
A turnaround can look cheap precisely because the problem is hard. Declining that kind of difficulty is a decision, not a lack of ambition.

Which "difficult problem" in your own portfolio would you be better off simply avoiding?
