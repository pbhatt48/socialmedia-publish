"Gentlemen who prefer bonds don’t know what they’re missing." — Peter Lynch, Beating the Street (1993)

Lynch put this among the "Peter's Principles" in Beating the Street. The joke has a serious point behind it.
A bond's return is capped by its coupon. A share of a good business grows as the business reinvests and earns more.
The cost of owning that growth is volatility, and the lines that look smoothest in the short run are often the flattest over decades.
Bonds still have their place, especially when yields are high relative to stocks. But price swings are not the same as permanent loss.

How do you decide how much of your portfolio belongs in businesses rather than fixed income?
