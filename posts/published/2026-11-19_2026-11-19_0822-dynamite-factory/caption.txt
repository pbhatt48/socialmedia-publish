"Choosing individual stocks without any idea of what you’re looking for is like running through a dynamite factory with a burning match. You may live, but you’re still an idiot." — Joel Greenblatt, The Little Book That Beats the Market

Greenblatt’s point is about process, not outcome. A good result from a bad process teaches the wrong lesson.
Before buying anything, an investor should be able to state what they are looking for: a cheap price, a good business, ideally both.
Criteria written down in advance protect you from the stories the market tells in the moment.
Surviving a reckless decision is luck. Repeating a sound one is a method.
