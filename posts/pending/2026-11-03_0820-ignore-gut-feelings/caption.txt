"The trick is not to learn to trust your gut feelings, but rather to discipline yourself to ignore them." — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street (1989)

Gut feelings in markets are rarely independent signals. They mostly echo the crowd, recent prices and the latest headline.
When prices have risen, the gut says buy. After they fall, it says sell. That timing is exactly backwards.
Lynch's alternative is to anchor on the company: hold while the fundamental story holds, and re-examine it when the facts change, not when the mood does.
Discipline here means a process written down in advance, so the decision is made before the emotion arrives.

What rule do you use to keep your gut out of your buy and sell decisions?
