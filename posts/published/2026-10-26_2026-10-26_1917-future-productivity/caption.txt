"Focus on the future productivity of the asset you are considering. If you don’t feel comfortable making a rough estimate of the asset’s future earnings, just forget it and move on." — Warren Buffett, 2013 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett illustrated this with a Nebraska farm and a New York retail property, bought for what they would yield, not for what someone might pay for them next week.
An asset is worth what it produces over its life. The quote on the screen is only someone else's opinion of that.
The second sentence matters as much as the first: if you can't sketch the earnings, you don't have an investment, you have a guess.
"Move on" is a skill in itself. Most of a good process is the pile of ideas you declined.

Which holding of yours could you not estimate earnings for, even roughly?
