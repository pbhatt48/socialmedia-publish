"Most investors, both institutional and individual, will find that the best way to own common stocks is through an index fund that charges minimal fees." — Warren Buffett, 1996 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

It is a striking sentence from the most celebrated stock picker of his era, and it is an honest one.
The arithmetic is simple: before costs, investors as a group earn the market’s return. After costs, the average investor must earn less. Fees are the one variable that is certain.
Buffett’s next line makes the point: those who follow this path are sure to beat the net results delivered by the great majority of investment professionals.
Picking businesses is a craft that demands time, temperament and a real edge. Owning the whole market cheaply demands only patience. Knowing which game you are actually equipped to play is itself a form of rational capital allocation.

Be honest: after costs and taxes, have your own selections beaten a low-cost index over the last ten years?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
