"The true investor scarcely ever is forced to sell his shares, and at all other times he is free to disregard the current price quotation." — Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor, Chapter 8

A quoted price is an offer, not an instruction.
Graham's point is that the investor's real advantage is optionality: no one can make you sell.
That advantage disappears the moment you let a falling quote dictate your behavior, or borrow so heavily that you can be forced out.
Keep the business in view, keep your balance sheet such that you are never a forced seller, and the daily tape becomes information you may use or ignore.

What would you have to change in your own portfolio to never be a forced seller?
