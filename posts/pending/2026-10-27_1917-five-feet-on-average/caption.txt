"Never forget the six-foot-tall man who drowned crossing the stream that was five feet deep on average." — Howard Marks, Memos to Oaktree Clients

An average describes a distribution. It does not describe the path you have to walk through it.
Investors live through sequences, not means. A strategy that works on average can still end you in the one year that matters.
This is why leverage, concentration and short-term liabilities deserve more scrutiny than expected returns do.
The first job is to survive the deep spots, so that the averages have time to work in your favor.

Which "average" in your own process might be hiding a deep spot?
