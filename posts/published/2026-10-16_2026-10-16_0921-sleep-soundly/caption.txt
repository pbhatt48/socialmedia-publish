"Ultimately, nothing should be more important to investors than the ability to sleep soundly at night." — Seth Klarman, Speech at MIT

Sleep is a surprisingly good test of how much risk you are carrying.
A portfolio you cannot hold through a bad quarter is not really a long-term portfolio. Eventually fear sells it for you, usually near the bottom.
Leverage, concentration you don't fully understand and positions sized beyond your conviction all show up first as restless nights.
A margin of safety does more than protect capital. It protects the temperament you need to stay rational when prices fall.

What position, if any, has ever cost you sleep, and what did it teach you about sizing?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
