"If the job has been correctly done when a common stock is purchased, the time to sell it is—almost never." — Philip A. Fisher, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (1958)

Notice the condition at the front of the sentence. Fisher's "almost never" is earned by the work done before the purchase, not granted by hope after it.
Deep research on management, growth runway, and competitive position is what makes patience rational rather than stubborn.
Every year brings a tempting exit: a full valuation, a scary headline, a shinier idea. Each sale resets the compounding clock and invites a tax bill.
Fisher still sold when the original thesis broke. The discipline is in knowing which is which.

What would have to change for you to sell your best holding?
