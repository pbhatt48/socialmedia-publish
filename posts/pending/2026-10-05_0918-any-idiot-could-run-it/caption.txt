"When somebody says, ‘Any idiot could run this joint,’ that’s a plus as far as I’m concerned, because sooner or later any idiot probably is going to be running it." — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street (1989)

Managers change. Talent leaves. Brilliance is not a durable asset.
A business that only works under a genius is a bet on the genius, not on the business.
Lynch’s test asks a harder question: what is left when ordinary people are in charge?
Simple economics, a clear product and a forgiving model are a margin of safety against management error.
Durability is often less about who runs the company than about how little it needs from them.

Which business you own could survive a mediocre CEO for a decade, and why?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
