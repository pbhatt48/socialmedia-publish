"The investor who says, ‘This time is different,’ when in fact it’s virtually a repeat of an earlier situation, has uttered among the four most costly words in the annals of investing." — Sir John Templeton, 16 Rules for Investment Success

Templeton's warning is not that the world never changes. It is that investors reach for novelty to excuse prices that history would never excuse.
Every mania arrives with a new story: new technology, a new era, a new way of measuring value.
The details really are different each time. Human behaviour, and the arithmetic of overpaying, are not.
The discipline is to ask what the earlier situation looked like, and how it ended, before deciding this one is exempt.
