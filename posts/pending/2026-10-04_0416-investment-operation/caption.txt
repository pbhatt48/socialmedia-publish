"An investment operation is one which, upon thorough analysis, promises safety of principal and an adequate return. Operations not meeting these requirements are speculative." — Benjamin Graham, Security Analysis (1934)

Graham's definition has three parts, and each one does work: thorough analysis, safety of principal, an adequate return.
Notice what it leaves out. Holding period, asset class and how respectable the security looks are not part of the test.
"Adequate" is a modest word. It asks for a sufficient return, not a spectacular one.
By this standard, buying a blue-chip stock without analysis can be speculation, and buying an unloved bond after careful work can be investment.
