"Value investing requires a great deal of hard work, unusually strict discipline, and a long-term investment horizon." — Seth Klarman, Margin of Safety (1991)

The idea itself fits on an index card: buy assets for less than they are worth.
Klarman's point is that the concept is not the scarce part. The temperament is.
Hard work finds the bargains, discipline refuses everything that isn't one, and a long horizon lets value be recognized on its own schedule.
Most investors have at least one of the three. Very few sustain all three through a full cycle.

Which of the three do you find hardest to maintain, and why?
