"Predicting rain doesn’t count; building arks does." — Warren Buffett, 2001 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this after September 11th, admitting he had recognized a large terrorism exposure at General Re and failed to act on it.
The lesson is uncomfortable: seeing a risk clearly is worth nothing until it changes how capital is positioned.
For investors, the ark is unglamorous: a balance sheet that can absorb a shock, reserves of cash, and positions sized to survive being wrong.
Forecasts are cheap and plentiful. Preparation is costly, and it must be paid for before the weather turns.

Which risk do you see clearly today but have not yet acted on?
