"In 58 years of Berkshire management, most of my capital-allocation decisions have been no better than so-so." — Warren Buffett, 2022 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

This comes from one of the best capital-allocation records in history, and he grades most of it as average.
In the same letter, Buffett says the results came from about a dozen truly good decisions, roughly one every five years.
The lesson isn't that every call has to be brilliant. It's that a few great ones, held for a long time, can carry everything else.
Mistakes are part of the record. What matters is that they stay small and the good decisions are left alone to compound.

If you graded your last ten investment decisions honestly, how many would you call truly good?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
