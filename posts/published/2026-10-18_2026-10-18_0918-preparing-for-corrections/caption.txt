"Far more money has been lost by investors preparing for corrections, or trying to anticipate corrections, than has been lost in corrections themselves." — Peter Lynch, Worth Magazine (1995)

Corrections are visible and memorable. The cost of sitting out to avoid them is quiet: it shows up only as the compounding that never happened.
To time a correction well you must be right twice, once getting out and again getting back in, and the second call is usually made in fear.
Lynch's alternative was to know the businesses you own well enough that a falling price reads as information about the market, not about the company.
The decline is a temporary cost. Missing the recovery is often a permanent one.

Have you ever held cash waiting for a pullback that never came at the price you wanted? What did it teach you?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
