"In this business if you’re good, you’re right six times out of ten. You’re never going to be right nine times out of ten." — Peter Lynch, PBS Frontline Interview

Lynch ran Magellan through one of the great records in fund management, and he still expected to be wrong four times in ten.
The point is not to lower standards; it is to design a process that survives its own error rate.
That means sizing so no single mistake is fatal, and letting the winners run long enough to pay for the losers.
An investor who demands to be right every time will either take no positions or defend bad ones far too long.

What is your honest hit rate, and does your process still work if it is lower than you think?
