"With few exceptions, when a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for poor fundamental economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact." — Warren Buffett, 1980 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this after years of watching turnarounds that rarely turned.
Talent matters, but it works inside the limits a business's economics set.
A great manager in a poor industry is a strong rower against a stronger current.
That is why the first question is about the business, and only then about the people running it.
Buffett learned this at Berkshire's own textile mills, which closed in 1985.

Can you think of a turnaround where good management truly beat bad economics? What made it work?
