"There’s a big difference between probability and outcome. Probable things fail to happen—and improbable things happen—all the time." — Howard Marks, The Most Important Thing

A sound decision can produce a bad result, and a reckless one can be rewarded.
Judging a choice only by how it turned out teaches the wrong lessons.
The investor’s job is to weigh the full range of outcomes, not to bet everything on the likeliest one.
Diversification, margin of safety and staying power exist for the tail you did not expect.

When did a well-reasoned decision of yours work out badly, and what did you conclude from it?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
