"Owning stocks is like having children — don’t get involved with more than you can handle." — Peter Lynch, Beating the Street (1993)

Lynch's point is about capacity, not courage. Every position you own is a business you have promised to keep understanding.
He suggested a part-time investor might follow eight to twelve companies, and that there's no need to own more than five at a time.
Beyond that number, attention thins out: earnings reports go unread, theses go untested, and a portfolio becomes a list of tickers.
Diversification protects you from what you don't know; it should not become an excuse for not knowing.

How many businesses could you honestly explain, in detail, right now?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
