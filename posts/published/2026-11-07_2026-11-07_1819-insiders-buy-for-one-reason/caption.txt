"Insiders might sell their shares for any number of reasons, but they buy them for only one: they think the price will rise." — Peter Lynch, One Up on Wall Street (1989)

Selling is noisy. An executive may sell to pay taxes, buy a home, fund tuition, or simply diversify.
Buying with personal capital, at market prices, is a far cleaner signal.
Executives see the order book, the pipeline, and the problems before anyone else does.
Still, a signal is not a thesis. It is a reason to look harder at the business, never a substitute for doing the work.

Do you track insider purchases in your own research, and how much weight do you give them?
