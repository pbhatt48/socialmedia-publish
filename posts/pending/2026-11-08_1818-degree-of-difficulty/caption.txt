"Investors should remember that their scorecard is not computed using Olympic-diving methods: Degree-of-difficulty doesn’t count." — Warren Buffett, 1994 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

In diving, a harder dive earns a higher multiplier. In investing, the market pays only for being right.
An elaborate model with a dozen moving parts earns nothing extra for its sophistication. It only adds more ways to be wrong.
Buffett's alternative: find a business whose value rests on one key factor that is easy to understand and likely to last.
Complexity can feel like rigor. Often it is just a bet that you got more things right.
