"Put together a portfolio of companies whose aggregate earnings march upward over the years, and so also will the portfolio’s market value." — Warren Buffett, 1996 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Prices are noisy in any given year. Over decades they are tethered to what the underlying businesses earn.
This shifts the investor’s question from “where will the stock go?” to “what will these companies earn in ten years, and how confident am I?”
Buffett tracked Berkshire’s “look-through” earnings for exactly this reason: the owner’s share of profits, whether or not they were paid out.
The quotation can drift above or below the line for long stretches. The line itself is what you are actually buying.

If you added up the earnings behind your holdings, would that number be higher five years from now?
