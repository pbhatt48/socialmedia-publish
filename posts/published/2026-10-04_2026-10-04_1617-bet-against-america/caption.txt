"For 240 years it’s been a terrible mistake to bet against America, and now is no time to start." — Warren Buffett, 2015 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this in an election year, against a loud chorus of national pessimism.
The long record includes wars, panics, depressions and deep drawdowns. None of them stopped the compounding.
Pessimism often sounds more intelligent than optimism, but the base rate has favored the optimist.
The discipline is to respect the setbacks without mistaking them for the trend.

What makes long-term pessimism so persuasive in the moment, even when history argues against it?
