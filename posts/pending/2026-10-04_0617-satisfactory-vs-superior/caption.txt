"To achieve satisfactory investment results is easier than most people realize; to achieve superior results is harder than it looks." — Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor

Graham chose this as the closing sentence of the book.
The first half is encouragement: a defensive investor who stays diversified, keeps costs low and avoids speculation can do well with modest effort.
The second half is a warning: beating the market demands an edge, and most people who chase one end up with neither.
The danger lies in the gap between the two, where extra activity adds risk and cost without adding skill.

Which kind of results are you honestly set up to achieve, and does your process match that answer?
