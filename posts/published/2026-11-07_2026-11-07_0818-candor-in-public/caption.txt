"We also believe candor benefits us as managers: The CEO who misleads others in public may eventually mislead himself in private." — Warren Buffett, Berkshire Hathaway Owner's Manual

Candor is usually framed as a duty owed to shareholders. Buffett frames it as a discipline that protects the manager.
A flattering story told often enough becomes the story one believes, and capital gets allocated on the story rather than the facts.
Writing plainly about mistakes forces you to see them, which is the first step to not repeating them.
For investors, the tone of a management letter is evidence: does it explain what went wrong as clearly as what went right?

Which annual letter have you read that was most honest about its own mistakes?
