"Investing is a popularity contest, and the most dangerous thing is to buy something at the peak of its popularity." — Howard Marks, “Random Thoughts on the Identification of Investment Opportunities,” Oaktree Memo, January 1994

By the time an asset is everyone’s favourite, the good news is already in the price.
At the peak, there are few new buyers left to push it higher, and plenty of holders ready to leave.
Popularity does not make a business worse. It makes the price harder to justify.
The bargains tend to sit where attention is thinnest.

Which widely loved asset today do you think is priced for perfection?
