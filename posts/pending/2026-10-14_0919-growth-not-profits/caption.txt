"Obvious prospects for physical growth in a business do not translate into obvious profits for investors." — Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor

Graham drew this lesson from the airlines: an industry that grew enormously while many of its shareholders lost money.
When a growth story is obvious, it is usually already in the price.
Competition flows toward visible opportunity and bids away the returns.
What matters is not how fast a business grows, but what you pay for it and what each new dollar of capital earns.

Which "obvious" growth story today do you think is already fully priced?
