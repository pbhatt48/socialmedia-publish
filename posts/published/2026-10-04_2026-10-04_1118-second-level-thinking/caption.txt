"Second-level thinking is deep, complex and convoluted." — Howard Marks, The Most Important Thing

Marks contrasts it with first-level thinking: good company, so buy the stock.
The second-level thinker asks what everyone else already believes, and what the price already assumes.
A great company at a price that bakes in perfection can be a poor investment. A mediocre one priced for disaster can be a good one.
Being right about the business is not enough. You have to be right where the consensus is wrong.

When you last bought something, what did you believe that the market didn't?
