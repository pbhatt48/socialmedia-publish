"After ten years on the job, a CEO whose company annually retains earnings equal to 10% of net worth will have been responsible for the deployment of more than 60% of all the capital at work in the business." — Warren Buffett, 1987 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

The arithmetic is quiet but decisive: 1.1 to the tenth power is about 2.59, so roughly 61% of the capital at work by year ten was put there by the current CEO.
In the same letter, Buffett notes that many CEOs rise through marketing, production, engineering or administration, not through capital allocation.
A decade into a tenure, the balance sheet is largely a record of one person's reinvestment choices.
That is why reading how management has deployed retained earnings can tell you more than reading its forecasts.

When you judge a CEO, how much weight do you give to how they have allocated capital, compared with how they run operations?
