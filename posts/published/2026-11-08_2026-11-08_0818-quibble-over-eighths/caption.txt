"Don’t quibble over eighths and quarters." — Philip A. Fisher, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (1958)

Fisher wrote when stocks traded in fractions of a dollar, and he saw investors lose great businesses over a few pennies.
The logic still holds. If a company can compound for decades, the difference between a limit price and the market price is noise.
Precision in the bid can mask a lack of conviction in the thesis.
Discipline on valuation matters. Haggling over the last tick is something else.

Have you ever missed a great business because your limit order sat just below the market?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
