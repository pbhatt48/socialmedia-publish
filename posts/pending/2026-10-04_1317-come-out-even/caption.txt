"More money has probably been lost by investors holding a stock they really did not want until they could ‘at least come out even’ than from any other single reason." — Philip A. Fisher, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (1958)

The market does not know or care what you paid. Your cost basis is a fact about you, not about the business.
Fisher traced the habit to ego: none of us likes to admit we were wrong.
He also counted the hidden cost, the profits the same capital could have earned if it had been redeployed when the mistake was first recognized.
The useful question is never "Can I get back to even?" but "Would I buy this today, at this price, with what I now know?"

Is there a position you are holding only because selling would make the loss feel real?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
