"Most investors strive fruitlessly for certainty and precision, avoiding situations in which information is difficult to obtain. Yet high uncertainty is frequently accompanied by low prices." — Seth Klarman, Margin of Safety (1991)

Certainty is never free. By the time a situation is easy to analyse, the discount has usually been competed away.
Klarman's point is not that uncertainty is good, but that the market charges for comfort.
The work is to separate what is unknowable from what is merely uncomfortable, and to size positions so that being wrong is survivable.
A margin of safety is what makes acting on incomplete information rational rather than reckless.

Where have you paid a premium simply to feel sure?
