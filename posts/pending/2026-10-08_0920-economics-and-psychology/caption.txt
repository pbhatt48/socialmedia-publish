"Investing is the intersection of economics and psychology." — Seth Klarman, Interview with Charlie Rose

The economics is the analysis: cash flows, balance sheets, competitive position, a range of reasonable values.
The psychology is everything around it: how much to buy, whether to wait, what to do when prices fall and the headlines turn dark.
Klarman's point is that the second half is usually the harder one. The spreadsheet does not panic; the person holding it might.
Most mistakes come not from bad arithmetic but from acting against your own arithmetic at the worst possible moment.

Which side gives you more trouble: valuing the business, or staying rational about it?
