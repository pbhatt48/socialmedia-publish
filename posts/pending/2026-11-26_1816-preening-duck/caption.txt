"In a bull market, one must avoid the error of the preening duck that quacks boastfully after a torrential rainstorm, thinking that its paddling skills have caused it to rise in the world." — Warren Buffett, 1997 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

When the tide comes in, every duck on the pond rises. The rise says little about the paddling.
Buffett applied the image to his own record: Berkshire had a strong year in 1997, but an index fund had done almost as well.
The honest question isn't "Did I make money?" It's "How did I do compared with the other ducks, and why?"
Separating the return the market handed you from the return your judgment earned is the start of real self-assessment.

How do you tell whether your results came from skill or from a rising market?
