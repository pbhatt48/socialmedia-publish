"Cash, though, is to a business as oxygen is to an individual: never thought about when it is present, the only thing in mind when it is absent." — Warren Buffett, 2014 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

In good times, idle cash looks like a drag on returns, and every spare dollar gets put to work.
Buffett wrote this with 2008 in mind, when long-prosperous companies suddenly wondered whether their checks would clear.
A strong balance sheet is not inefficiency; it is the price of never being a forced seller or a forced borrower.
And the reserve does double duty: the cash that keeps you alive in a panic is also what lets you buy when others cannot.

How much dry powder do you hold, and what would make you spend it?
