"Investment success doesn’t come from ‘buying good things,’ but rather from ‘buying things well.’" — Howard Marks, The Most Important Thing

Marks separates two questions that investors constantly blur: how good is the asset, and how good is the price.
A wonderful company bought at a price that already assumes perfection can be a poor investment.
A mediocre asset bought cheaply enough can be a fine one.
The quality of a business is a fact about the business; the quality of an investment is a fact about the price you paid for it.

When you last bought something, were you judging the asset or the price?
