"Operations for profit should be based not on optimism but on arithmetic." — Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor

Graham's test is simple: before committing capital, the numbers must work on their own, without a hopeful story to carry them.
Optimism lets us skip the calculation; arithmetic forces us to state what we are paying, what we are getting, and what could go wrong.
A thesis that only works if everything goes right is not an investment. It is a wish with a ticker attached.
The discipline is unglamorous: earnings, assets, cash flows and a price, checked against each other.
