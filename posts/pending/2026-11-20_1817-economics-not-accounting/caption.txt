"Accounting consequences do not influence our operating or capital-allocation decisions." — Warren Buffett, 1983 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett followed it with a striking preference: at similar cost, he would rather buy $2 of earnings Berkshire could not report than $1 it could.
Accounting is a set of conventions for describing a business. It is not the business itself.
Managers who steer by reported numbers often trade real value for a smoother-looking income statement.
For the owner, what matters is the cash a business can eventually produce and how well it is redeployed.

Where have you seen a company sacrifice economic value to make its reported earnings look better?
