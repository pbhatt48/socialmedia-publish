"I learned to go into business only with people whom I like, trust, and admire." — Warren Buffett, 1989 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett listed this among the mistakes of Berkshire's first twenty-five years: a lesson learned, not assumed.
Contracts and covenants cannot fully protect you from a partner you don't trust; character does most of the work.
For an investor, buying a stock is choosing a partner. Management's candor and capital decisions will shape your returns long after the purchase price is forgotten.
Three tests, all required: liking makes the relationship durable, trust makes it safe, admiration makes it worth having.

Which of the three do you find hardest to judge from the outside: like, trust, or admire?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
