"You won’t improve results by pulling out the flowers and watering the weeds." — Peter Lynch, One Up on Wall Street (1989)

Lynch was describing a habit he saw everywhere: sell the stock that has gone up, and keep the one that has fallen until it comes back.
It feels prudent, because it locks in a gain and puts off admitting a loss. In practice it trims the businesses that are working and keeps money in the ones that aren't.
A price move on its own tells you nothing about whether to sell. The question is whether the company's story has improved or deteriorated.
Judge each holding on its prospects from here, not on whether it is above or below what you paid.

Which is harder for you: holding a winner that keeps rising, or selling a loser whose thesis has broken?
