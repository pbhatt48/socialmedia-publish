"We think the very term “value investing” is redundant. What is “investing” if it is not the act of seeking value at least sufficient to justify the amount paid?" — Warren Buffett, 1992 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this to reject the neat split between “value” and “growth” styles.
Growth is not a rival philosophy; it is one input into what a business is worth, and it can add value or destroy it.
A low multiple does not make a purchase an investment, and a high one does not rule it out. The only question is whether what you receive justifies what you pay.
Paying more than your estimate of value in the hope of selling higher has another name: speculation.

When you buy a stock, can you state what you think it is worth, and why?
