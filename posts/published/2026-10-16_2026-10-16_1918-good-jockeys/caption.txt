"Good jockeys will do well on good horses, but not on broken-down nags." — Warren Buffett, 1989 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this looking back on Berkshire's textile mills and the Hochschild, Kohn department store: both were run by able, honest people, and neither could escape its economics.
Investors often underwrite the manager and overlook the business. Talent can lift returns at the margin; it rarely overcomes a structurally poor industry.
The order of questions matters: first, is this a good horse? Only then, is the jockey worthy of it?
A great operator on a strong franchise compounds. A great operator on a weak one mostly fights erosion.

Have you ever owned a business where excellent management couldn't overcome poor economics?
