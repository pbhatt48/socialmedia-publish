"The speculator’s primary interest lies in anticipating and profiting from market fluctuations. The investor’s primary interest lies in acquiring and holding suitable securities at suitable prices." — Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor, Chapter 8

Graham drew the line between investor and speculator by attitude, not by the security owned.
The same stock can be an investment for one holder and a speculation for another.
The speculator needs to be right about what other people will do next. The investor needs to be right about what the business is worth and what is being paid for it.
To the investor, fluctuations are not the goal. They are occasional chances to buy below value, or to sell above it.

Looking at your own holdings, which ones are you holding for the business, and which for the next move in price?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
