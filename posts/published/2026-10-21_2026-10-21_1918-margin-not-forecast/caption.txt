"The function of the margin of safety is, in essence, that of rendering unnecessary an accurate estimate of the future." — Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor, Ch. 20

Graham did not ask investors to predict better. He asked them to need prediction less.
Any estimate of value is a guess about earnings, rates, and competition years from now, and guesses miss.
Paying well below that estimate turns the forecast from a requirement into a rough guide: a wide range of outcomes can still end in an acceptable result.
The less the price leaves room for error, the more precisely right you have to be.
