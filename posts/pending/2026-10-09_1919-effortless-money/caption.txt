"Nothing sedates rationality like large doses of effortless money." — Warren Buffett, 2000 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this as the dot-com bubble was deflating, describing how a long run of easy gains blurs the line between investment and speculation.
The danger is not ignorance. It is that success which required no effort feels like proof that no effort is needed.
Every rising price quietly lowers the bar for the next decision, until valuation stops being asked about at all.
The antidote is a process that does not care how recent results felt: the same questions about cash flows, price and downside, asked in good markets as in bad.

When your portfolio has done well without much work, what check do you use to make sure your judgment hasn't gone to sleep?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
