"Every decade or so, dark clouds will fill the economic skies, and they will briefly rain gold. When downpours of that sort occur, it’s imperative that we rush outdoors carrying washtubs, not teaspoons." — Warren Buffett, 2016 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

The best prices tend to appear exactly when buying feels most uncomfortable.
Seeing the opportunity is the easy part. Having the cash, the liquidity and the nerve to act at scale is the hard part.
That readiness is built in the calm years: avoiding leverage, holding reserves that look idle, and knowing in advance what you want to own.
A teaspoon is what you carry when you were not prepared, or when fear has shrunk your conviction.

What are you doing today so you can carry a washtub in the next downturn?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
