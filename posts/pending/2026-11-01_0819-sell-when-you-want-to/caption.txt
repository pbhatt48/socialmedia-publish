"The trick of successful investors is to sell when they want to, not when they have to." — Seth Klarman, Margin of Safety (1991)

Klarman follows the line with a blunt rule: investors who may need to sell should not own marketable securities other than U.S. Treasury bills.
A forced seller takes whatever price the market offers on the worst day. A patient owner can wait for value to be recognized.
The ability to wait is not mainly temperament. It is structure: modest leverage, cash set aside for known needs, money that is genuinely long-term.
Most investment disasters are not bad analysis. They are good positions held by people who could not afford to keep holding them.

What in your own setup could force you to sell at the wrong time?
