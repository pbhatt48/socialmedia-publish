"It’s not given to human beings to have such talent that they can just know everything about everything all the time. But it is given to human beings who work hard at it—who look and sift the world for a mispriced bet—that they can occasionally find one." — Charlie Munger, 1994 USC Business School Lecture

Munger starts with humility: no one sees everything, and pretending otherwise is how investors get hurt.
Then comes the work. The edge lies in sifting patiently, then saying no to almost all of it.
Notice the word "occasionally." Mispriced bets are rare, and the discipline is in waiting for one rather than forcing one.
Most of investing is the sifting. The finding happens only now and then.

How many ideas do you look at before you find one worth acting on?
