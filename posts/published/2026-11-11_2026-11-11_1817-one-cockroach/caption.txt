"In a difficult business, no sooner is one problem solved than another surfaces – never is there just one cockroach in the kitchen." — Warren Buffett, 1989 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this while reviewing the mistakes of Berkshire's first 25 years, starting with buying a textile mill because it looked cheap.
A bargain price does not fix a bad business. The problems are structural, so they keep returning in new forms.
Management energy spent patching one leak is energy not spent compounding.
The better path is to own businesses where problems are rare and small, not businesses that need constant rescue.

Have you ever owned a "cheap" stock where every fix revealed a new problem?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
