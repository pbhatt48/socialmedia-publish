"Skepticism and pessimism aren’t synonymous. Skepticism calls for pessimism when optimism is excessive. But it also calls for optimism when pessimism is excessive." — Howard Marks, “The Limits to Negativism,” Oaktree Memo, October 2008

Marks wrote this in mid-October 2008, when the crowd had swung from believing nothing could go wrong to believing nothing could go right.
Skepticism is often mistaken for permanent gloom. Its real job is to doubt whatever the consensus takes for granted.
In a boom, that means questioning the good news. In a panic, it means questioning the bad news.
The discipline is symmetrical. Most investors only practise it in one direction.

When did you last apply your skepticism to the pessimists rather than the optimists?
