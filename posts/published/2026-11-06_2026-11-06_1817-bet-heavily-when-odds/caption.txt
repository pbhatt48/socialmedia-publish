"The wise ones bet heavily when the world offers them that opportunity. They bet big when they have the odds. And the rest of the time, they don't. It's just that simple." — Charlie Munger, A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom (USC, 1994)

Munger built this on the racetrack's pari-mutuel system: the crowd sets the odds, so mispriced bets are rare.
The edge is not in being active. It is in knowing what you understand and declining everything else.
Doing nothing for long stretches is not idleness. It is how you keep the capital and the conviction for when the price is clearly wrong.
And when that moment comes, a timid position wastes it.
