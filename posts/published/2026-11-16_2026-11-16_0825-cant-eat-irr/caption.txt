"You can’t eat IRR." — Howard Marks, “You Can’t Eat IRR,” Oaktree Memo (2006)

A rate of return is a percentage. What you can spend is dollars.
A 40% return held for one year turns $100 into $140. An 18% return compounded for eight years turns it into roughly $376.
The higher IRR wins the headline, but the lower one earns about seven times the profit.
Duration matters: how much capital you can deploy, and how long it can stay at work.
Judge an investment by the wealth it creates, not by how impressive the percentage looks.

When you size up an opportunity, do you ask “what rate?” or “how much, for how long?”

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
