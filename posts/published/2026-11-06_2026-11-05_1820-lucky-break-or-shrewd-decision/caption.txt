"One lucky break, or one supremely shrewd decision—can we tell them apart?—may count for more than a lifetime of journeyman efforts." — Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor, Postscript

Graham wrote this about GEICO: a single concentrated position, a departure from his usual principles, whose profits exceeded those of twenty years of wide-ranging operations combined.
The founder of disciplined value investing admitted he could not cleanly separate luck from skill in his greatest result.
His conclusion was not that discipline is pointless. Behind the break, he wrote, there must usually exist a background of preparation and disciplined capacity.
The journeyman years are what let you recognize the rare opportunity, and act on it with size, when it arrives.

How do you judge whether a great result in your own record came from skill or from luck?
