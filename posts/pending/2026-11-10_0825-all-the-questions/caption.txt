"An investor who has all the answers doesn’t even understand all the questions." — Sir John Templeton, 16 Rules for Investment Success (1993)

The more a business is understood, the longer the list of things that could still go wrong with it.
Certainty is usually a sign that the hard questions have not yet been asked: about competition, capital needs, management and price.
Humility is not timidity. It is what keeps an investor checking the thesis, sizing positions sensibly, and leaving room for error.
The best analysts tend to keep a running list of what they do not know, and keep it honest.

What question about a company you own do you still not have a good answer to?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
