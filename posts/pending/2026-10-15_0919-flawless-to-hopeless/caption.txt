"In the real world, things generally fluctuate between ‘pretty good’ and ‘not so hot.’ But in the world of investing, perception often swings from ‘flawless’ to ‘hopeless.’" — Howard Marks, "On the Couch," Oaktree Memo, January 2016

Marks wrote this as markets were sliding into early 2016, and he has returned to it in later memos because it keeps proving true.
Businesses rarely change as much as their stock prices suggest. What changes is the story investors tell about them.
The opportunity lives in the gap between the two: buying when "hopeless" is priced in, being cautious when "flawless" is assumed.
That takes the discipline to judge the business, not the mood around it.

Which company do you think the market is treating as flawless, or hopeless, today?
