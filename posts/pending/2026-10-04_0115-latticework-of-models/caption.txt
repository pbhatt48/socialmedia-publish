"You’ve got to have models in your head. And you’ve got to array your experience — both vicarious and direct — on this latticework of models." — Charlie Munger, 1994 Talk at USC Business School

Isolated facts are trivia. Connected facts become judgment.
Munger’s point: borrow the big ideas from many disciplines — psychology, math, economics, biology — and hang every lesson on that frame.
A single model breeds the “man with a hammer” problem. Several models let you see a business from more than one angle.
Most of what you learn will be vicarious: other people’s wins and mistakes, absorbed cheaply.

Which mental model has improved your investment decisions the most?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
