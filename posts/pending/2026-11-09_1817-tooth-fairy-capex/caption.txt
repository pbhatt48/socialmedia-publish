"References to EBITDA make us shudder — does management think the tooth fairy pays for capital expenditures?" — Warren Buffett, 2000 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Depreciation is not a fiction. It is the bill for machinery, buildings and systems that wear out and must be replaced.
A metric that adds it back describes cash the owners can never actually take out of the business.
Owner earnings ask the harder question: what remains after the spending required just to stand still?
When a management team leads with a flattering number, it is worth asking which cost it was designed to hide.

Which line on the cash flow statement do you check first when a company leads with adjusted figures?
