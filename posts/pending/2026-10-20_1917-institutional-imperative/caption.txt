"Any business craving of the leader, however foolish, will be quickly supported by detailed rate-of-return and strategic studies prepared by his troops." — Warren Buffett, 1989 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett called this the institutional imperative, and listed it among the biggest surprises of his first twenty-five years running Berkshire.
The problem is rarely venality or stupidity. Analysis that is meant to test a decision gets recruited to justify one that has already been made.
For owners, a polished projection is not evidence. What matters is a capital allocator who will let the numbers say no.
Look at how past acquisitions turned out against the case made for them at the time. The record of returns tells you more than the next presentation.

When you study a management team, how do you tell genuine analysis from analysis built to order?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
