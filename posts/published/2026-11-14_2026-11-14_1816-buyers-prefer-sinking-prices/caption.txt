"Only those who will be sellers of equities in the near future should be happy at seeing stocks rise. Prospective purchasers should much prefer sinking prices." — Warren Buffett, 1997 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett framed it as a quiz: if you plan to eat hamburgers all your life and don't raise cattle, should you hope for higher or lower beef prices?
Most people answer correctly about hamburgers and then cheer when the stocks they will keep buying get more expensive.
A saver who adds to equities for decades is a net buyer, and a net buyer is helped, not hurt, by lower prices.
The same logic applies to a company buying back its own shares: a lower price means each dollar retires more ownership.

Over the next decade, will you be a net buyer or a net seller of stocks, and does your reaction to falling prices match that answer?
