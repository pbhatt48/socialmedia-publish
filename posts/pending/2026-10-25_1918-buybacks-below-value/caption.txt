"When companies with outstanding businesses and comfortable financial positions find their shares selling far below intrinsic value in the marketplace, no alternative action can benefit shareholders as surely as repurchases." — Warren Buffett, 1984 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Notice the conditions Buffett attaches: an outstanding business, a comfortable balance sheet, and a price far below intrinsic value. Remove any one and the case weakens.
When all three hold, a buyback is simply buying more of a business you already know best, at a discount, for the owners who stay.
Retire shares at a fair or inflated price and the arithmetic turns against those same owners. Price decides whether a repurchase builds value or destroys it.
Judge buybacks by what was paid, not by how many shares disappeared.

When a company you own announces a repurchase, how do you decide whether management is buying below value?
