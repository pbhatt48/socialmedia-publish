"Your investor’s edge is not something you get from Wall Street experts. It’s something you already have." — Peter Lynch, One Up on Wall Street (1989)

Lynch’s point was not that amateurs are smarter than professionals. It was that they often see things first.
The nurse who notices which devices hospitals keep reordering, the engineer who knows which supplier never misses a spec, the shopper who sees the line out the door: that is information, gathered years before it shows up in a research report.
An edge only counts if it is real, specific, and acted on with homework. Noticing a good store is the beginning of the analysis, not the end of it.
The discipline is to stay inside what you genuinely understand, and to treat that knowledge as an advantage rather than an anecdote.

What does your daily work let you see about a business or industry before the market does?
