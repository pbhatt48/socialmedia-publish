"I thought then that decent, intelligent, and experienced managers would automatically make rational business decisions. But I learned over time that isn’t so. Instead, rationality frequently wilts when the institutional imperative comes into play." — Warren Buffett, 1989 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett called it his most surprising discovery: bad decisions often come not from venality or stupidity, but from institutional dynamics.
He listed its forms. Organizations resist changing direction. Projects and acquisitions materialize to soak up available funds. A leader's cravings get supported by studies from the troops. Peers get mindlessly imitated.
For an investor, this is a lens on management: does the company spend cash because it is there, or because the return justifies it?
The rarest managers are the ones willing to look different from their peers when the arithmetic says so.

Where have you seen the institutional imperative override plain good sense?
