"Understanding both the power of compound interest and the difficulty of getting it is the heart and soul of understanding a lot of things." — Charlie Munger, Poor Charlie’s Almanack

Most people can recite the arithmetic of compounding. Far fewer respect what it demands.
The math needs a high rate of return, sustained for decades, with no large interruption along the way.
Each of those conditions is hard to meet, and fees, taxes, turnover and impatience quietly erode all three.
The power is obvious only at the end; the difficulty is felt every single year before it.

Which do you find harder to secure in practice: a high return, or the long, uninterrupted runway?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
