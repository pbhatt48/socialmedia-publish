"Inactivity strikes us as intelligent behavior." — Warren Buffett, 1996 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Activity feels like diligence, but in investing it is mostly cost: commissions, taxes, and the steady leak of decisions made on noise.
Buffett's point is not that one should stop thinking. It is that once you own a wonderful business at a sensible price, the work is largely done.
No owner would sell a great private company because a pundit changed his mind about interest rates. Minority shares deserve the same patience.
The hard part is psychological: doing nothing has to be a choice made with conviction, not a default born of neglect.

What was the last trade you made that you would not have made if you had simply waited a year?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
