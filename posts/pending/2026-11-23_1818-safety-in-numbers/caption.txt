"Diversify. In stocks and bonds, as in much else, there is safety in numbers." — Sir John Templeton, 16 Rules for Investment Success

Templeton was a deep contrarian, yet he never confused conviction with certainty.
Diversification is an admission that any single judgment can be wrong, however careful the analysis behind it.
The aim is not to own everything, but to ensure that no one mistake can end the game.
A portfolio built this way can afford the occasional failure and still let its winners compound.

How do you decide when concentration becomes fragility in your own portfolio?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
