"The primary test of managerial economic performance is the achievement of a high earnings rate on equity capital employed (without undue leverage, accounting gimmickry, etc.) and not the achievement of consistent gains in earnings per share." — Warren Buffett, 1979 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Earnings per share can rise every year for a business that is standing still.
Retain the profits, add them to the capital base, and even a savings account shows "growth."
Buffett's test asks what each dollar of equity actually earns, not how smooth the line looks.
Leverage and accounting can flatter a return; they cannot make a mediocre business a good one.
Judge management by the rate it earns on what it already holds.

Which company you follow reports steady EPS growth while its return on equity quietly declines?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
