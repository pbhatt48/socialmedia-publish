"Should you find yourself in a chronically-leaking boat, energy devoted to changing vessels is likely to be more productive than energy devoted to patching leaks." — Warren Buffett, 1985 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this the year he closed Berkshire's textile mills, after two decades of capable managers and fresh capital failed to overcome the industry's economics.
The lesson is that a business's economics usually matter more than the effort of the people running it.
Patching leaks feels productive, and every patch can be justified on its own. Added together, they keep capital tied up in a boat that is still sinking.
For an allocator, the hardest part is often admitting which boat you are in.

Have you ever held on to a "leaking boat" too long? What finally made you change vessels?
