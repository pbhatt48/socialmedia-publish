"Earnings can change dramatically in a short time. Usually assets change slowly." — Walter Schloss, Factors Needed to Make Money in the Stock Market, 1994

Schloss compounded for nearly five decades largely by buying assets at a discount, not forecasts of next year's profit.
An earnings estimate is a guess about the future; a balance sheet is a record of what already exists.
Buying earnings demands that you know a great deal about the business. Buying assets cheaply leaves more room to be wrong.
The trade-off: assets are only worth what they can earn or fetch, so the discount must be real, not just reported.

When you value a company, how much weight do you give the balance sheet versus the income statement?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
