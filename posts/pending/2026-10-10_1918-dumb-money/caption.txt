"Paradoxically, when “dumb” money acknowledges its limitations, it ceases to be dumb." — Warren Buffett, 1993 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Buffett wrote this right after observing that a know-nothing investor who buys an index fund periodically can actually outperform most investment professionals.
The edge he describes is not information or intelligence. It is an honest estimate of what you do not know.
An investor who admits they cannot value individual businesses, and so owns many of them and spaces out purchases, avoids the costly errors that come from false confidence.
Self-knowledge is a form of risk management. Knowing where your skill ends matters more than how far it reaches.

Where do you draw the line between what you can analyze yourself and what you should simply own broadly?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
