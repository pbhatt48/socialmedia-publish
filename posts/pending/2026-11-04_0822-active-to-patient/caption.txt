"Our stay-put behavior reflects our view that the stock market serves as a relocation center at which money is moved from the active to the patient." — Warren Buffett, 1991 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Activity feels like diligence, but each trade carries costs: commissions, spreads, taxes, and the chance to be wrong again.
The patient owner pays those costs rarely and lets the business do the compounding.
Markets reward the ability to sit still through noise more reliably than the ability to react to it.
Doing nothing well is a skill: it rests on having bought something you understand at a sensible price.

When did you last sell a good business too early, and what pushed you to act?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
