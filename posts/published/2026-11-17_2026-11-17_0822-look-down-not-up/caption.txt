"Look down, not up, when making your initial investment decision. If you don’t lose money, most of the remaining alternatives are good ones." — Joel Greenblatt, You Can Be a Stock Market Genius (1997)

Most investment theses begin with the upside: how big this could get, what it could be worth.
Greenblatt reverses the order. First ask what you could lose, and how likely that is.
The arithmetic of losses is unforgiving: a 50% decline needs a 100% gain just to get back to even.
Protect the floor, and the range of outcomes left over is mostly favorable.
The upside is hard to quantify, but it tends to take care of itself.

Before your last purchase, did you write down the bear case as carefully as the bull case?
