"The stock market demands conviction as surely as it victimizes the unconvinced." — Peter Lynch, One Up On Wall Street (1989)

Lynch's point is that the investor who doesn't know why they own something will sell it at the worst moment.
Conviction is not stubbornness; it is the residue of work done before the price moved.
A thesis you can state in a few sentences is one you can test against the facts, not against the quote screen.
When the facts change, change your mind. When only the price changes, reread your reasons.
Borrowed conviction tends to be the first thing a falling market takes away.

What is one holding you could defend in three sentences without mentioning its share price?
