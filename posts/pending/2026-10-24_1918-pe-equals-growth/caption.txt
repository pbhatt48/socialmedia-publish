"The p/e ratio of any company that’s fairly priced will equal its growth rate." — Peter Lynch, One Up on Wall Street (1989)

This is the seed of the PEG ratio, and Lynch offered it as a rule of thumb, not a law.
Its value is the question it forces: what am I paying for each point of growth?
A high multiple is not expensive in itself, and a low one is not cheap in itself. Both only mean something next to what the business can actually deliver.
The hard part is the denominator: growth is an estimate, while the price you pay is a fact.

When you look at a multiple, how do you decide how much future growth to believe?
