"Wall Street sometimes gets confused between risk and uncertainty, and you can profit handsomely from that confusion." — Mohnish Pabrai, The Dhandho Investor (2007)

Uncertainty is a wide range of possible outcomes. Risk is the chance of permanent loss of capital. They are not the same thing.
Markets dislike not knowing, so businesses with murky near-term prospects are often priced as though the worst case were the likely one.
When the price already sits close to a conservative floor of value, a wide spread of outcomes can coexist with very little downside.
Pabrai's framing is the coin toss: heads, you win; tails, you don't lose much.

Think of a stock you avoided because the future looked unclear. Was it truly risky, or only uncertain?
