"Your goal as an investor should simply be to purchase, at a rational price, a part interest in an easily-understandable business whose earnings are virtually certain to be materially higher five, ten and twenty years from now." — Warren Buffett, 1996 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Every clause is a filter: a rational price, a part interest, a business you can understand, earnings you can reasonably foresee.
Buffett added that few companies meet these standards, which is why patience matters more than activity.
"Virtually certain" sets a high bar. It excludes most of what is exciting and much of what is popular.
The work is less about forecasting markets and more about judging durability.

Which of these filters do you find hardest to apply in practice?
