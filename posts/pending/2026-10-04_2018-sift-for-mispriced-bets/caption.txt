"It’s not given to human beings to have such talent that they can just know everything about everything all the time. But it is given to human beings who work hard at it—who look and sift the world for a mispriced bet—that they can occasionally find one." — Charlie Munger, “A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom,” USC Business School, 1994

Munger is describing the job as he saw it: searching patiently, not knowing everything.
No one can understand every company, every industry, every market at every moment. That's not the requirement.
The requirement is diligence: reading widely, looking at many things, and rejecting most of them.
Most of the time the sieve catches nothing. The edge comes from the rare occasion it does, and being ready to act.

How many ideas do you look at and reject for every one you actually buy?

Learn. Think. Allocate. Compound.
