"Managers that always promise to ‘make the numbers’ will at some point be tempted to make up the numbers." — Warren Buffett, 2002 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

Real businesses do not earn in straight lines. Weather, cycles, competitors and plain luck make results lumpy.
So a record of hitting guidance quarter after quarter should raise a question rather than settle one.
When the promise becomes the goal, the pressure builds quietly: pull revenue forward, stretch an estimate, defer a cost.
Buffett and Munger said plainly that they did not know what Berkshire would earn next quarter. That candor is itself a signal of quality.

When you read a CEO's letter, do you trust the smooth story or the honest one more?
