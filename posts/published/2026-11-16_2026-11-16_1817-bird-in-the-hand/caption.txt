"The formula for valuing all assets that are purchased for financial gain has been unchanged since it was first laid out by a very smart man in about 600 B.C. (though he wasn’t smart enough to know it was 600 B.C.)" — Warren Buffett, 2000 Letter to Berkshire Shareholders

The very smart man was Aesop, and the formula is "a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush."
Buffett turns the proverb into three questions: how certain are you that the birds are in the bush, when will they emerge, and how many will there be?
Stocks, bonds, farms and businesses are all valued the same way: by the cash they will deliver, discounted for time and uncertainty.
Valuation tools come and go, but none of them changes the underlying arithmetic.

Which of the three questions do you find hardest to answer for the businesses you own?
