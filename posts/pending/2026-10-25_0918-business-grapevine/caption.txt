"The business ‘grapevine’ is a remarkable thing." — Philip A. Fisher, Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (1958)

This line opens Fisher’s chapter on what he called “scuttlebutt”: research done by talking to the people around a business rather than only reading its reports.
Customers, suppliers, competitors, former employees and researchers in the field each see one side of a company. Put together, those views often tell you more than a polished annual report.
Competitors are especially revealing. People who would never criticize their own employer will often speak candidly about a rival’s strengths and weaknesses.
Financial statements show what has already happened. The grapevine can hint at what is happening now.

Who outside a company, a customer, supplier or competitor, has taught you the most about a business you own?
